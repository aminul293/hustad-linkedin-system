# How Hustad actually services a client

Written because a draft in this system once had a crew dispatched to a roof leak at two in the
morning. Roofing does not work that way, and copy that gets it wrong is worse than copy that says
nothing. Anything drafting in Eric's name, human or scheduled job, reads this before writing about
service delivery.

## The sequence, in order

**1. Baseline inspection, before anything happens.** Every roof in the portfolio is inspected up
front, section by section. Each section is photographed, graded, and every deficiency found is
priced. Drains and penetrations are checked. Warranty checkpoints are logged. The result sits in
the client portal before the first service call ever comes in.

The grade runs a nine point scale: A, A minus, B, B minus, C, C minus, D, D minus, F. It reflects
roof life remaining rather than age, and it sets the replacement window used in capital planning.
Findings are split urgent (active or about to fail) and preventive (the same kind of problem caught
early). Both are documented with photos at inspection.

**2. A ticket gets created two ways.** The client calls their Hustad account manager, or the client
opens a service ticket themselves in the portal. Those are the two front doors.

**3. Dispatch happens in the morning.** Nobody puts a roofing crew on a roof in the dark. A leak
reported overnight is dispatched the next morning. Write it that way. The value of the program is
that the morning dispatch starts from a documented condition rather than from scratch, not that
somebody answers a phone at 2 a.m.

**4. Repairs run against a preapproved cap.** The annual NTE authorization is sized per property
from roof area and building count. Small items get repaired during the inspection visit instead of
waiting for a separate approval cycle and a second mobilization. The cap is a ceiling, never a bill,
and unused authorization is never invoiced. Exceeding a cap requires written approval, with one
exception: emergency stabilization for safety or active damage prevention never waits.

**5. Everything is documented the same way on every property.** Daily photos, time stamped notes,
safety checklists, all in the portal. Real time status, site history, and A to F grades by building.

**6. Reporting runs on a fixed rhythm.**

| Cadence | What the client gets |
|---|---|
| Monthly | NTE usage and closeout summaries, open items with aging and priorities, service ticket trends |
| Quarterly | Regional risk review and patterns, repeat issue root causes, budget forecast updates |
| Annually | Full inspection reports by property, CapEx recommendations and priority, lifecycle strategy |

Every closeout carries an inspection report with photos, findings and recommended actions; the
itemized NTE closeout; the warranty checkpoint log; and a budget ready summary written for the
director or regional who has to approve something.

## The four buckets every finding lands in

From the Sales Brain, section 18. This is the vocabulary to use when writing about what happens to
a finding, and it is what answers "temporary versus permanent" questions from operators.

- **Emergency.** Active leak, exposed system, high probability of immediate interior damage.
- **Proactive.** Documented condition not failing inside yet, but likely to create repeat service
  or shorten useful life.
- **Capital.** Repair no longer buys enough certainty; the system is entering replacement planning.
- **Monitor only.** Not enough evidence to repair now; worth watching. Say what would trigger action.

## Open item aging

Section 17. Closed work orders matter. Aged open items matter more, because aging shows where the
process is slowing and where ownership is losing control.

| Age | What it means |
|---|---|
| 0 to 30 days | Still active and manageable |
| 31 to 60 days | Decision drag forming |
| 61 to 90 days | Budget risk forming |
| 90 plus days | Control is slipping |

## Onboarding, when someone says yes

A three week pilot. Week one is the kickoff call, data exchange and site access. Week two is on site
inspections with same day photos and notes in the portal. Week three is the pilot review: findings,
NTE repairs completed, and plan recommendations. From there the decision is whether to extend the
cadence portfolio wide.

## What not to claim

No pricing in a DM or a post. No claim outcome or deductible promises; coverage is always subject
to the carrier and the policy. Warranty terms are stated generally ("often", "usually", "generally
not covered"), never as absolutes, because terms vary by manufacturer. The published KPI medians
(inspection to proposal, on time completion, punch list closeout) live in the cleared intro deck;
confirm they are current before repeating them anywhere.

And the standing rule: Hustad works on buildings that already stand. No ground up construction, no
owner's representation on a new build, no warranty setup at turnover.
